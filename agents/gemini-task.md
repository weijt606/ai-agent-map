One-line take: Task is the agent mode of the Gemini app — introduced at I/O as **Gemini Spark**, a cloud agent on Gemini 3.5 and the [Antigravity](antigravity.md) harness — which runs scheduled jobs across Google Workspace, Chrome and connected apps, with a published list of what it will ask you to confirm first.

## When To Pick It

- Your life already runs on **Gmail, Docs, Slides and the rest of Google Workspace**: Google built it to integrate with those tools first-party.
- You want **schedules**: tasks can run on a schedule, and you can pause and resume a schedule from the thread. Up to 15 tasks run at once.
- You want to watch it work: it shows the plan, its progress, and the files and connected apps it used.

## When Not To Pick It

- You are in the EEA, Switzerland, the UK or Nigeria, or on a work account.
- You do not want activity retained: Keep Activity must be on.
- You want it to finish purchases or enter payment details unattended. By design it hands those steps back to you.

## Permissions And Data

Google's help page lists what it asks to confirm: sending communications, modifying your data, making purchases, submitting web forms, and signing in with Google. For passwords or payment details it pauses and asks you to **take control** of the browser. Since July 30, it can use your logged-in accounts and saved passwords "with your permission", starting in the US. Google's own risk notes are blunt: Gemini can share information from your chats, connected apps and Personal Intelligence **with the websites it visits**, and with local Chrome it has access to every site you are signed into. Remote browser data and code-execution history can be deleted in Task settings.

## Bottom Line

The safest default for a Google-centric life — clear confirmations, visible plans, first-party Workspace access — at a mid-range price, but closed to Europe and dependent on settings that keep your activity on file.
