One-line take: Cue is Manus's standalone app for personal agents in which **each agent gets its own email address, phone number, wallet and computer** — so it can message people, pay within a budget you set and take your calls — which makes it the agent on this list with the widest reach beyond your own accounts.

## Why This Entry Exists

Every other agent here borrows *your* identity: it signs in as you, sends from your mailbox, pays with your card. Cue gives the agent **an identity of its own**. That is a different design decision with different consequences, and it is worth having on the map as the far end of the spectrum.

## When To Pick It

- You want an agent that can deal with the outside world **without exposing your own inbox, number or card**: it sends from its own email and number and pays from its own wallet within your budget.
- You want call screening with a written summary — it can take your calls and leave you one.
- You want several agents on one goal: put them in a group chat and they hand work to each other.

## When Not To Pick It

- You need a price, a model or a region commitment: none is stated first-hand yet.
- You cannot get an invite — access is limited and first-come, first-served.
- You are not comfortable with an agent that **acts as a separate party** toward other people. Messages and payments from the agent's own identity are still sent on your instruction, and the people on the other end may not know they are dealing with an agent.

## Permissions And Data

Manus's launch post says you set the budget and the direction; it does not document an approval model, retention or training terms for Cue. The App Store listing links to Manus's general privacy policy. Treat this as an early-access product whose governance is not yet written down. (Update, Oct 28 2026: the App Store description now says Cue completes "purchases and payments with your approval" — "You confirm the payment" — that automations "can run on a schedule or respond to supported events and conditions", and that "Memory and Dream" keep preferences; retention and training terms are still not stated.)

## Bottom Line

The most distinctive design on the list — the agent as its own party, with its own phone and wallet — and one of the least documented. Worth trying on low-stakes errands; not yet something to give a real budget without terms in writing.
