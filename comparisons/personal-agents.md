Agents you hand your day to — errands, inboxes, bookings, documents, your phone — sold as consumer products rather than run as software. None of them is open source (one has an open-source sibling). This page collects them, compares them on facts and control rather than popularity, and logs what changes.

> **Last reviewed:** 2026-11-11 · Every fact below comes from the vendor's own page, help center or app-store listing unless it is marked *media*. A cell reading **?** means the vendor does not document it first-hand — not that the product lacks it.

## Why There Is No Popularity Ranking Here

The rest of this map ranks open-source projects by GitHub stars, a public number anyone can check. Closed consumer products have no equivalent: downloads, monthly users and app-store ranks are reported second-hand, on different definitions, if at all. A ranked list built on them would look precise and be made up. So this page **lists and compares** instead, and the "Recent changes" log at the bottom is where movement shows up.

For self-hosted personal agents you run yourself — [Hermes Agent](../agents/hermes-agent.md), [OpenClaw](../agents/openclaw.md), [OpenHuman](../agents/openhuman.md), [Goose](../agents/goose.md) — and the desktop knowledge-work agents [Kimi Work](../agents/kimi-work.md) and [WorkBuddy](../agents/workbuddy.md), see the [capability matrix](../capabilities/matrix.md) and the heat ranking.

How the hard cells were scored: **Your device** is ● only where the agent operates apps on a phone you hold (Doubao's system service; AutoGLM's open framework), ◐ where it can use your computer or browser, ○ where it works only on a vendor machine. **Approval controls** is ● where the vendor documents per-action confirmation rules or scoped grants (dots' automatic review and custom rules, Gemini's confirmation list, Claude's three modes, Duet's send/pay/sign/delete approval and off-limits list, Muse's Sentinel), ◐ where control exists but is partial or coarse (Grok Bot's Auto Review does not cover every side effect; Cue's budget and per-payment confirmation; Doubao's app-level SAEP declarations; Today says only that "key steps" such as submitting forms, ordering and paying are yours to confirm or take over, and that you can take over or stop at any time).

## Three Questions That Decide Most Choices

1. **Whose identity does it act under?** Almost every agent here signs in as you. [Cue](../agents/manus-cue.md) gives each agent its own email, phone number and wallet — less exposure for your accounts, more for the people it contacts.
2. **Whose machine does it work on?** A vendor cloud computer (dots, Duet, Muse, Grok Bot, Cue, AutoGLM's app, Today), your browser or computer (Gemini, Claude, dots' connected PC, Today's Chrome extension), or your phone's operating system (Doubao). The closer to your device, the larger the permission you are granting.
3. **Can the vendor see it, and does it train on it?** Muse's operator can still reach your data until its announced Confidential VM ships; Claude's training is an opt-in choice with 5-year vs 30-day retention; Grok Bot excludes training only under Privacy Mode; Gemini Task requires Keep Activity on; both Today editions state that identifiable user content is not used to train public foundation models, and the China edition stores data in mainland China in principle, with real-time voice possibly processed in Singapore under separate consent. Several products do not state this at all — treat that as an answer.
