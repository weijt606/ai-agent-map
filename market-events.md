# Market Events

[English](market-events.md) | [中文](zh/market-events.md)

Structural events that reshaped agent selection — model releases, product mergers, and waves — newest first. The weekly play-by-play lives in the "Market events" timeline in [agents/README.md](agents/README.md); this page keeps the durable records.

## October 28 – November 10 2026 — Fable 5.2 Ships, Gemini 4 Carbon Reaches The API, Kimi K3.1 Pro Goes Open, And The Default-Model Freeze Ends

**Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2 on November 9** (`claude-fable-5-2`), with outputs up to 30% faster than 5.1 and an optional fast mode; Claude Code's changelog gives a 1M context and Pi's release notes a 128K output limit. On Anthropic's own table, Fable 5.2 scores **69.9% on FrontierSWE v2** and **49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0** (Xhigh effort), against 62.3% and 45.5% for Opus 5.5, 65.5% and 42.8% for [GPT-6 Astra](agents/gpt-6-astra.md), and 61.2% and 41.1% for Gemini 4 Carbon (its FrontierCode score at Medium effort). These are vendor-reported, on benchmarks this map has not tracked before. Anthropic still recommends starting most coding and knowledge work on Opus 5.5. Blocked requests are now answered by Opus 4.8 for cybersecurity and Opus 5 for biology, and Mythos 5.2 goes to organizations in Anthropic's Cyber and Life Sciences Verification Programs, and Claude Enterprise customers can use it through Claude Security. [Claude Code](agents/claude-code.md) `2.1.321` made Fable 5.2 the default Fable model the same day and moved saved Fable 5.1 picks to the `fable` alias. GitHub Copilot added it for Pro+, Max, Business and Enterprise.

**Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28**, "our strongest model with state-of-the-art cybersecurity and long horizon capabilities", at first only for a subset of cyber defenders through its Fairwind Program. It reached the Gemini API as `gemini-4-carbon` on **November 10** and is rolling out to Google AI Ultra in Antigravity, the Gemini app (including Gemini Task) and AI Mode. Google reports #1 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0 (59); that is not the Coding Agent Index this map tracks, and Google's coding table is an image this map could not read. **This map missed the October 28 announcement**: the 11-04 edition said nothing about it, and the benchmark table had no Carbon row until now. GitHub Copilot added Carbon on November 10.

**Kimi K3.1 Pro's weights are public.** `moonshotai/Kimi-K3.1-Pro` is on Hugging Face, not gated, with a second commit dated November 5 07:11 UTC; Hugging Face does not show the moment it went public, and this map's November 4 fetch found it private. The model card gives 2.8T total and 104B activated parameters and a 1M context, and repeats Moonshot's Terminal-Bench 4.0 score of 65.7 (Opus 5.5 66.4, GPT-6 Astra 58.2). The **Kimi K3.1 Pro License** is MIT-style, with two conditions: a Model-as-a-Service business above a revenue threshold must sign a separate agreement, and products above 100 million monthly active users or a monthly revenue threshold must display "Kimi K3.1-Pro" prominently. It is now in the [open-weights table](comparisons/benchmarks-and-billing.md#the-open-weights-tier).

**OpenAI released GPT-6-Cyber on November 5** (`gpt-6-cyber`), for investigating vulnerabilities, reproducing them in an authorized environment and verifying fixes; the changelog says to see its Daybreak setup before the first request. It is not a coding default.

**A second December 1 reroute this map had not recorded:** Google's November 2 Gemini API entry, the same one that routes `gemini-2.5-pro` to `gemini-3.8-flash`, also routes `gemini-2.5-flash` to `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`; the 11-04 edition reported only the first. [Gemini CLI](agents/gemini-cli.md)'s source default is still `gemini-2.5-pro` at `v0.68.0` (Nov 10), and its docs now say it "has not been optimized for Gemini 4 Argon" and point Argon users to Antigravity.

**Two licence and product changes in tracked repos.** anthropics/skills moved its Office skills (`docx`, `pdf`, `pptx`, `xlsx`) from source-available to Apache-2.0 on November 6 (PR #2146), the same Apache-2.0 licence as most other skills there; the [Agent Skills](comparisons/agent-skills.md) page is corrected. [Paperclip](agents/paperclip.md) `v2026.1106.0` makes its native Runner the default for new Claude, Codex, OpenCode and Pi agents and removes Pipelines.

**Consumer agents moved too.** Carbon reached Gemini Task on Ultra, [ChatGPT dots](agents/chatgpt-dots.md) added GPT-6.1 Terra, [Muse](agents/meta-muse.md) added voice and Meta's "specialized versions" of its models, and [Grok Bot](agents/grok-bot.md)'s docs now say Grok Bot chooses the model; the [personal agents](comparisons/personal-agents.md#recent-changes) log has the detail.

**Impact on selection:** two things.

First, **the default-model freeze is over, but inside families.** For five windows no tracked coding agent changed a default in a release. On November 9 two did: Claude Code moved its Fable default to 5.2, and [Pi](agents/pi.md) `v1.5.1` moved its `anthropic` provider default from Opus 4.8 to Opus 5.5 and moved several other providers to newer models. The model a new Claude Code session starts on, and [Codex CLI](agents/codex.md)'s GPT-6.1 Sol, did not change. If you had picked Fable 5.1 in Claude Code, your pick now follows the `fable` alias, which points to 5.2; `/model claude-fable-5-1` switches back.

Second, **the new ceiling is not the new starting point.** Anthropic's own announcement says to start on Opus 5.5 and step up to Fable, and Carbon reached the API 13 days after its announcement. The choice at the top stays per task, as it has been since Fable 5 shipped.

## October 27 – November 4 2026 — Copilot Moves Toward Routing And Metering, GPT-6 Rosalind Stays Out Of Coding, And Kimi's Weights Are Still Pending

**GitHub made Copilot Fusion generally available on October 28.** You choose Fusion and Copilot "intelligently" routes and orchestrates the models it uses; administrators can set it as the default for Copilot users through enterprise managed settings, so this is not a vendor-wide default change. GitHub says Fusion Max "scored 60, within two points of Claude Code with Opus 5.5 Max" on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index. That is GitHub's report of a third-party index; this map has not copied it into the benchmark table. The same day **Copilot became included with GitHub Enterprise** for enterprises working with a GitHub or Microsoft account team, with AI usage metered from the first request and no included allowance. Self-serve follows "in stages", and Copilot Business and Enterprise remain available.

**OpenAI released GPT-6 Rosalind on October 30**, "specialized biological reasoning with scientific tool use", for organizations approved for its trusted-access program. It is not a coding model.

**Kimi K3.1 Pro's weights were not public when this edition was fetched on November 4** (11:16 Beijing time), the date Moonshot gave ("by Nov 4"), so the day is not over: its Hugging Face path is not public. Its quickstart has replaced the `[xx.x%]` placeholders with scores: Moonshot reports 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, against 66.4% for Claude Opus 5.5 and 58.2% for GPT-6 Astra, and 61.2% on its own Kimi Code Bench 3.0 Long-horizon. Those are the vendor's numbers, and it stays out of the open-weights table until the weights ship. **Google will route `gemini-2.5-pro` to `gemini-3.8-flash` from December 1** (changelog, November 2); that string is still [Gemini CLI](agents/gemini-cli.md)'s `DEFAULT_GEMINI_MODEL` in source, so its effective default may change without a release. Anthropic deprecated fast mode for Opus 4.8, with removal on November 30.

**Anthropic opened message threads in beta on the Messages API on November 2**: the API keeps the conversation on the server, so each later request sends only the new messages, and a thread expires 5 minutes after its last request.

**Consumer agents moved too.** [Muse](agents/meta-muse.md) opened in Singapore on October 27, and [ChatGPT dots](agents/chatgpt-dots.md) added iMessage and RCS texting in beta for US Pro users; the [personal agents](comparisons/personal-agents.md#recent-changes) log has the detail.

**Two earlier releases this map had not recorded.** **MiniMax M3.1 Flash** reached MiniMax's API on October 19; its post said "Model weights will be released later", and its Hugging Face repository now lists a custom `minimax-community` licence and a vendor-reported Terminal-Bench 4.0 score of 38.1. **Grok 4.7** reached the xAI API on September 21, with a 500K context window.

**Impact on selection:** two things.

First, **the Copilot question is shifting from seats to metering.** A flat seat no longer covers every way to buy Copilot, and Fusion takes the model choice away unless you keep it. If you buy through an enterprise agreement, ask which of the two you are on.

Second, **nothing moved on defaults.** No tracked coding agent changed its default model in the window, the fifth window running ([Claude Code](agents/claude-code.md) `v2.1.312`–`v2.1.318`; [Codex CLI](agents/codex.md) `rust-v0.165.0` and `rust-v0.166.0` add GPT-6.1 Terra to the catalogs with Sol still first; Gemini CLI `v0.66.0`; [OpenCode](agents/opencode.md) `v2.2.1`–`v2.2.5`, whose "New Default Model Picker" is a UI change).

## October 21–26 2026 — GPT-6.1 Terra Ships, Kimi K3.1 Pro Arrives Without Scores, And Anthropic Donates Agent Skills And Files To List

**OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra on October 26 2026**, "a fast, efficient model for visual reasoning, precise computer use, and clear, natural writing". It has a 1.05M context (922K max input, 128K output). The same day it became available in Codex and ChatGPT Work on Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu plans, and in GitHub Copilot. It is **not a default**: Codex `rust-v0.164.0` (Oct 23) still bundles GPT-6.1 Sol first, and the Codex changelog entry makes Terra selectable only. OpenAI's launch article was not readable to this map, so no benchmark is recorded.

**Moonshot released Kimi K3.1 Pro**, dated October 24 on its own research index (OpenCode announced availability on October 23). Moonshot calls it "our most capable 3T-class open-weight model to date", a post-trained Kimi K3 with a 1M context, and says "the full model weights will be released by Nov 4". It is on Moonshot's API, with a Highspeed variant at 2–3× the speed. Its quickstart still carries **unfilled benchmark placeholders** (`[xx.x%]` for Terminal-Bench 4.0), so this map records no score and does not add it to the open-weights table until the weights and numbers are published. Moonshot also says K2-series models close to newly registered users from **October 31** and retire by the end of December 2026.

**Anthropic donated Agent Skills to the Agentic AI Foundation on October 22**, stating that more than 70 agent platforms, including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code, have integrated the standard since December 2025, and that 1 million skills have been published (both vendor-stated). On **October 26 it filed an S-1** with the SEC for a proposed IPO of Class A common stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol "ANT"; share count and price range are not yet set. It also opened a waitlisted research preview of **[Claude Duet](agents/claude-duet.md)** (October 21), a separate personal-agent app, profiled this refresh and recorded on the [personal agents](comparisons/personal-agents.md#recent-changes) page.

**OpenCode's October 17 incident, recorded last window without a vendor statement, now has one on record.** The vendor's X post says the breach was "related to a large scale distillation attack that impacted 1.4% of OpenCode Console users", the subset "who had external providers linked", all notified by email, with remediation under way with Anthropic, OpenAI and Google. No GitHub advisory or status-page incident was published. Users report in issues that OpenCode's own keys were revoked too; the vendor has not confirmed that.

**Impact on selection:** two things.

First, **a model without numbers is not yet a choice.** Kimi K3.1 Pro has a context window and a weights date, and its own document says its scores are not written yet. Treat it as a watch item until Nov 4.

Second, **Agent Skills moving to a foundation changes who owns the format the skills wave is built on**, not how it works today. The collections this map tracks ([Superpowers](agents/superpowers.md), [mattpocock/skills](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills), [anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills)) already ship to that standard. No tracked coding agent changed its default model in the window — the fourth window running ([Claude Code](agents/claude-code.md) `v2.1.307`–`v2.1.311`, [Codex CLI](agents/codex.md) `rust-v0.164.0`, [OpenCode](agents/opencode.md) `v2.2.0`, [Qwen Code](agents/qwen-code.md), [Kimi Code](agents/kimi-code.md); [Gemini CLI](agents/gemini-cli.md) shipped no stable release).

A larger **Muse Wonder Full** is not on the API; it is available only inside the Muse Code subscription, on the Power and Maximum usage plans. **Meta released the weights of Muse Spark Open on October 15 2026**: 816B total parameters, 42B active, a 1M-token context, natively multimodal, under the **Muse Model License "with limited commercial terms"**. Meta calls it "the most capable US open-weight model to date" and optimized it to run through MLX on Mac Studio M5 Ultra. **DeepSeek shipped DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 on October 17**, a point update it describes as stronger on benchmarks, faster and shorter in output than V4.1-Flash, "a Pareto improvement", with weights published; it names [DeepSeek Harness](agents/deepseek-harness.md) `v0.2.1-rc.1` or later as the client to use, and the harness's `rc.2` (same day) added support for it.

It sits in the same review-first route as [Open Code Review](agents/open-code-review.md) and [Cline](agents/cline.md), as a managed product rather than a CLI.

**Impact on selection:** two things.

First, **a model retirement inside an agent product is a configuration risk, not just a lineage note.** Anyone who pinned `gpt-5.5` in a ChatGPT-signed-in Codex workspace — as this map has advised for defaults — now has a pin that resolves to nothing. Pin, and also put model retirements on a review calendar.

Second, **the open-weights tier gained its first US entrant on a custom licence.** Muse Spark Open joins Kimi K3 in the bespoke-licence column of the [open-weights table](comparisons/benchmarks-and-billing.md#the-open-weights-tier); read "limited commercial terms" before the parameter count. ## September 28–29 2026 — Sonnet 5.5 And GPT-6.1 Sol Become The Defaults In One Week

**Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5.5 (`claude-sonnet-5-5`) on September 28 2026**. Anthropic's framing is a faster Sonnet rather than a stronger one: 30%+ faster than Sonnet 5, matching Sonnet 5 on coding "while using significantly fewer steps, tokens, and tool calls". Its published table has one number that stands out against the rest of the family: **Terminal-Bench 4.0 70.6%**, above the **66.4%** published for [Opus 5.5](#september-22-2026--opus-55-and-gpt-6-sol-and-luna-ship-on-the-same-day) six days earlier. On every other benchmark the two share, Sonnet 5.5 trails Opus 5.5 (CursorBench 4.0 55.5% against 57.8%, FrontierCode v1.1 46.2% against 54.4%, Humanity's Last Exam with tools 64.5% against 67.7%, GDPval-AA v2.1 1844 against 1846 Elo). Anthropic says **Haiku 5.5** "will join the Claude 5.5 family in the coming weeks".

**[Claude Code](agents/claude-code.md) `2.1.284` made Sonnet 5.5 the default Sonnet model the same day**, stating the 1M context in its changelog; Anthropic sets the default effort to Medium in Claude Code and its apps.

**The next day, at OpenAI's DevDay, GPT-6.1 Sol arrived.** **[Codex CLI](agents/codex.md) `rust-v0.159.1` (Sept 29) made GPT-6.1 Sol the default in its bundled catalog** and in the Amazon Bedrock catalogs, replacing the [GPT-6 Astra](agents/gpt-6-astra.md) default it had set on September 4. DevDay also brought reusable Codex cloud environments, a refreshed Codex CLI, a new code-review experience and computer use in the Agents API; those are product-surface changes this map will pick up in the [Codex](agents/codex.md) profile rather than here.

**Impact on selection:** two things.

First, **both vendors now point their own coding agent at the middle tier.** In September the map recorded four default-model changes inside a coding agent's patch release — Codex to Astra (Sept 4), Claude Code to Opus 5.5 (Sept 22), Claude Code to Sonnet 5.5 (Sept 28), Codex to GPT-6.1 Sol (Sept 29). The last two move *down* the ladder. If you run either agent on its defaults, what you are running changed this week without a major version.

Second, **a Sonnet out-scoring an Opus on the agentic-terminal benchmark is a reason to test the lower tier first**, not a reason to believe it is the stronger model overall — on every other shared benchmark it trails. It does strengthen the case this map has been making since July: for most coding work, start at the tier below the one you think you need.

## April – September 2026 — The Open-Weights Tier Reaches The Frontier, And Ships What The Closed Tier Gates (backfilled)

Four releases, read together for the first time on this map. **[DeepSeek V4](agents/deepseek-v4.md)** arrived in April under **MIT** — 1.6T parameters with 49B active, 1M context, SWE-bench Verified 80.6 — with a 284B/13B Flash variant beside it. **[Kimi K3](agents/kimi-k3.md)** followed in July: 2.8T parameters, the first open 3T-class model, natively multimodal with a MoonViT-V2 encoder in-model, 1M context, under a bespoke *Kimi K3 License*. **[GLM-5.3](agents/glm-5.md)** shipped under **Apache-2.0**, positioned by Zhipu as the most capable open-weights coding model. **Impact on selection:** two consequences, and the second one is the story.

First, **the open tier's real axis is licence, not capability**. MIT, Apache-2.0, a bespoke model licence, and per-checkpoint terms are four materially different legal positions at broadly comparable capability — so on this route the licence column decides more than the benchmark column. Deployment class is the tiebreaker after that: 2.8T and 1.6T parameters are clusters, ~3B activated is a workstation.

Second, and recorded here because this map watched the closed side do the opposite in the same window: **Zhipu states that GLM-5.3 is state of the art on CyberGym for vulnerability discovery, with its largest gains further up the exploitation chain, where it more than doubles GLM-5.2.** In the same weeks, [GPT-6 Astra](agents/gpt-6-astra.md)'s generally available version shipped **refusing part of its own cybersecurity capability** with more behind Daybreak Blue approval, and Anthropic's Mythos tier stayed restricted to vetted organizations through Project Glasswing. The capability the closed vendors are gating is downloadable under Apache-2.0.

This map takes no position on whether that should be so. The selection consequence is concrete and belongs in every evaluation on this route: **on the open tier, the gate is your policy rather than the vendor's**, which moves real work into your harness's approval design — see [observability and evals](comparisons/observability-and-evals.md) and the approval columns in the [capability matrix](capabilities/matrix.md). It also means defenders evaluating these models have exactly the access attackers do, which is the argument the open side makes for itself. Both things are true at once, and a selection map that reported only one of them would be doing propaganda rather than documentation.

Meta did not close the field; it joined one this map had mis-counted. The paragraph below is left as written, with this note, rather than silently rewritten. The vendor field filling out in under a month, mostly with agents you cannot read, is the actual story — and per the correction above, it was fuller than this entry realized at the time. Meanwhile the Claude Code and GPT-5.6-Cyber releases point the same direction from a different angle: the differentiator vendors are now competing on is **deployment and access control** — whose infrastructure the loop runs on, and who is allowed to run it — not the loop. Muse Code's replay-exact event log is the one genuine capability idea in the batch, and it is the same durability problem the [observability layer](comparisons/observability-and-evals.md) attacks from outside. Index figures for Muse Spark 1.2 are not yet recorded here, so it does not appear in [benchmarks & billing](comparisons/benchmarks-and-billing.md).

Sources: [Introducing Muse Code and Muse Spark 1.2](https://research.meta.ai/blog/introducing-muse-code-and-muse-spark-1-2), [developer.meta.com/ai/models/muse-spark](https://developer.meta.com/ai/models/muse-spark/), [Self-hosted environments for Claude Code](https://claude.com/blog/run-claude-code-sessions-on-your-own-compute), [Claude Code self-hosted docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/self-hosted-environments), [GPT-5.6-Cyber](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.6-cyber).

## July–August 2026 — The Meta-Harness Appears

Two projects landed the same idea from opposite ends within weeks of each other. **[QM](agents/qm.md)** (`yc-software/qm`, MIT) — Y Combinator's *multiplayer* agent for Slack and the web — reached **~11.4k stars and 1.3k forks in its first seven days**, and **[Omnigent](agents/omnigent.md)** (`omnigent-ai/omnigent`, Apache-2.0) passed 8k. Neither ships an agent loop. Both are layers that run *other* harnesses — Pi, OpenCode, Codex, Claude Code, Cursor, Hermes — behind one interface, and add what a bare loop leaves out: identity, approval policy, spend caps, sandboxing, scheduling, and session continuity.

**Impact on selection:** for two years the harness question was "which one do I fork." These projects assume the answer is "several, and that is fine" — which is a bet that the loop itself is commoditizing and the durable value sits in the governance layer above it. If that bet is right, "which harness" becomes a reversible decision and the important choice moves up a level. Note the two split cleanly on unit of account: QM's is an **organization** (a scope per person and per room, one admin-set security posture, self-hosted in your own cloud), Omnigent's is a **developer** (one session following you across terminal, browser, phone, and desktop, across nine cloud sandbox providers). Also note the maturity: QM is days old and Omnigent is self-declared alpha, so this is a shape worth tracking, not yet a layer worth depending on. QM carries the same governance caveat the map flagged for Grok Build in a different form — the license is MIT, but contributions are accepted as *written proposals*, not code. Details: [QM](agents/qm.md), [Omnigent](agents/omnigent.md), [agent harness frameworks](comparisons/agent-harness-frameworks.md).

Sources: [yc-software/qm](https://github.com/yc-software/qm), [qm.ycombinator.com](https://qm.ycombinator.com), [omnigent-ai/omnigent](https://github.com/omnigent-ai/omnigent), [omnigent.ai](https://omnigent.ai).

## June 17 2026 — Vercel Ships eve, And The Build-Your-Own Route Grows A Delivery Surface

Vercel released **[eve](agents/eve.md)** (`vercel/eve`, Apache-2.0) at Vercel Ship in London, as part of a set of products it groups under **Agent Stack**. The framing is "Next.js for agents": an agent is a *directory of files* — `instructions.md`, `tools/`, `skills/`, `subagents/`, `channels/`, `schedules/`, `connections/` — and the framework owns durable execution (checkpointed sessions on the open-source Workflow SDK that survive crashes *and* redeploys), a per-agent sandbox, `needsApproval` gates that pause a run indefinitely without consuming compute, subagents with isolated context windows, `defineEval` test suites, and channel adapters for Slack, Discord, Teams, GitHub, Linear, Telegram, Twilio, and HTTP. It reached **4.8k stars** in its first ten weeks. **This map missed it at the time and is recording it now as a backfill**, alongside the profile.

**Impact on selection:** eve changes the shape of the build-your-own route rather than just lengthening it. Every other framework on that route ([LangChain](agents/langchain.md), [LangGraph](agents/langgraph.md), [CrewAI](agents/crewai.md), [LlamaIndex](agents/llamaindex.md)) scores **—** on delivery surfaces in the [capability matrix](capabilities/matrix.md), because they are libraries and where the agent *shows up* is your problem. eve is the first entry there with channels, scheduling, and approvals as framework primitives — which makes it the closest thing on the build side to what the [meta-harnesses](agents/qm.md) do on the operate side. The cost is on the last column: it is the only framework on that route whose documented production path runs through one vendor's platform (`vercel deploy`, sandboxes and model access via AI Gateway), with self-hosting undocumented. Apache-2.0 makes the code portable; it does not make the deployment portable. That is a clean, explicit trade — batteries in exchange for a platform — and it is the whole decision.

Sources: [Introducing eve](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-eve), [vercel/eve](https://github.com/vercel/eve), [eve.dev](https://eve.dev/), [Vercel debuts eve](https://www.theregister.com/devops/2026/06/19/vercel-debuts-eve-open-source-agent-framework-tries-to-fix-shadow-ai-with-passport/5258726).
